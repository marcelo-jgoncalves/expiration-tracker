# Stage 1, Round 1 — Claude's BLIND independent proposal: grading criteria for the P0 screen-inventory plan

Written before reading any Codex output on this topic (anti-anchoring discipline, `AGENTS.md` §4,
same protocol as D-184/D-225/D-243). Goal of this document: define the RUBRIC that Stage 2's
actual screen inventory will later be graded against — not the screens themselves.

## Research basis (real sources, accessed 2026-09-08)

- [Navigation UX Best Practices For SaaS Products — Pencil & Paper](https://www.pencilandpaper.io/articles/ux-pattern-analysis-navigation) — persistent primary navigation, shallow hierarchy (any feature reachable in 2-3 clicks), breadcrumbs for location/hierarchy in deep IA.
- [Top RBAC providers for multi-tenant SaaS in 2025 — WorkOS](https://workos.com/blog/top-rbac-providers-for-multi-tenant-saas-2025) — RBAC in 2025 B2B SaaS must be tenant-aware (organizations/workspaces as first-class), not just static global roles.
- [10 Usability Heuristics Applied to Complex Applications — NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-heuristics-complex-applications/) — Nielsen's heuristics (visibility of system status, user control/freedom incl. undo, consistency, error recovery) apply directly to complex/enterprise applications, not just consumer software.
- [How to Conduct a Heuristic Evaluation — NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-conduct-a-heuristic-evaluation/) — heuristics are evaluation criteria against a design, supporting a graded-rubric approach rather than pass/fail.
- [Multi-Tenant Dashboard Design: A Guide for Enterprise SaaS — Orbix](https://www.orbix.studio/blogs/multi-tenant-dashboard-design) — four governing principles for multi-tenant UI: tenant isolation as a visual contract, role-aware information hierarchy, permission states that hold under load, consistent cross-tenant UX.
- [Build a multi-tenant SaaS application — Logto/Medium](https://logto.medium.com/build-a-multi-tenant-saas-application-a-complete-guide-from-design-to-implementation-d109d041f253) — the organization/workspace switcher is a small control with outsized power: it determines which reality every other screen renders; a wrong-workspace action should be structurally hard, not just visually different.
- [The Ultimate Design Handoff Checklist — Jenny Choi/Medium](https://medium.com/@designer.jennychoi/the-ultimate-design-handoff-checklist-every-developer-wishes-you-had-part-1-77858070b5d8) and [Design Handoff Checklist for Teams — DeveloperUX](https://developerux.com/2026/05/16/design-handoff-checklist/) — every screen needs its loading/empty/error/success states documented explicitly (not inferred), annotated with specific behavior, not just visual comps.

**Honesty note (discipline established by D-225's "no equivalent Box/Notion/Figma source found")**: I did not find an authoritative, named industry checklist that scores an entire *pre-build* screen-inventory plan end-to-end (as opposed to scoring a shipped UI via heuristic evaluation, or scoring a handoff file's completeness in isolation). The rubric below is my own synthesis of the sources above applied to the specific artifact this repo needs (a plan document, not a UI), which is why this Stage exists as a Claude↔Codex-graded exercise rather than a citation lookup.

## Proposed rubric — 7 weighted axes, 100 points total

| # | Axis | Weight | What "10/10" looks like |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | **IA completeness / no orphan features** | 20 | Every `Action` in `authorization.ts` (both modules' full surface) maps to at least one screen where it is exercised, or is explicitly named as deliberately deferred with a reason. Every domain entity with a CRUD/lifecycle surface (Document, Requirement, DocumentType, RequirementTemplate, DocumentRequest/Series, Organization, Membership, Invitation, TrackedSubject, RequirementAssignment, ExpirationItem, NotificationPreferences/Entitlements) has a discoverable home. |
| 2 | **RBAC-aware navigation** | 15 | For every screen, the plan states which of OWNER/ADMIN/MEMBER/VIEWER can see it and which actions each role can actually invoke there (not just "logged in"). A VIEWER-only nav never shows an entry point to an action VIEWER cannot perform (e.g., no dangling "Invite member" button for a VIEWER) — degrade to read-only or hide, decided explicitly per screen, not left ambiguous. |
| 3 | **State coverage per screen** | 15 | Every screen names its loading/empty/error/success states AND any domain-specific states (e.g., guest link expired/revoked, OCC conflict, guest anti-enumeration collapse, import job partial failure) — not generic "handle errors" hand-waving. Distinguishes empty-genuinely vs. empty-filtered vs. empty-permission where relevant (this repo's own prior planning, `interface-screen-and-state-inventory.md`, already established this distinction — the new plan should not regress from it). |
| 4 | **Multi-tenant & guest/authenticated surface separation** | 15 | Org switcher/tenant-context handling specified if relevant; a structural (not just visual) separation between the authenticated tenant-member app and the unauthenticated guest surface (guest never sees tenant nav, tenant screens never leak to guest); onboarding/invitation acceptance flow placed correctly relative to both. |
| 5 | **Screen-to-screen coherence (entry/exit graph)** | 15 | Every screen names its entry points (what links here) and exits (what it links to) such that the full graph has no orphan screen (unreachable from any nav or flow) and no dead-end screen (no way forward except back). Cross-references are consistent both directions (if A links to B, B's "entry points" lists A). |
| 6 | **Mobile/responsive & accessibility expectations** | 10 | Plan states, per screen or per class of screen, the responsive expectation (full parity, degraded/read-only, or explicitly desktop-only with reason) and calls out any interaction pattern requiring special mobile handling (e.g., guest upload from a phone camera, dense tables). Not a generic "must be responsive" statement. |
| 7 | **Self-contained buildability for a downstream tool** | 10 | A reader with zero session context (Claude Design) can build each screen without asking a clarifying question about what data it shows, who can act on it, or what happens next — because the plan restates the relevant backend facts inline or via unambiguous pointers, per screen, not just in a shared glossary the reader must cross-reference for every field. |

**Convergence gate**: same as this repo's established convention (D-184/D-225/D-243/`AGENTS.md` §4) — both Claude and Codex must independently score the rubric itself ≥9.0/10 on rigor (not the screens; the criteria), without rounding, before Stage 2 begins.
