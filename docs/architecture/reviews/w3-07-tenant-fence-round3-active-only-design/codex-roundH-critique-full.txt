
**DECISION: APPROVED WITH CONDITIONS.**

Sole architectural/product condition: an explicit human decision on the SES deletion policy must be recorded before implementation is considered approved:

- either block every send not yet submitted to SES once the tenant leaves `ACTIVE`;
- or permit already-admitted sends to drain under the specifically documented lease/capability policy and residual-risk statement.

The five-round review-only track has reached what it can close.

## 1. VERIFYING → DELETED — CLOSED

Yes: the combined solution resolves the Round G race without relabelling it.

The crucial improvement is separating three different properties:

- indefinite prevention of new admission, supplied by the DynamoDB lifecycle fence;
- exhaustion of already-admitted capabilities, supplied by the finalization cutoff;
- detection and repair of unexpectedly late residue, supplied by the post-`DELETED` sweeper.

The 1800-second value is no longer pretending to bound SQS, DLQ, or scheduler delivery.

That is correct because delayed delivery does not imply delayed authorization.

After `DELETING`, those invocations may execute indefinitely late, but their mutations remain rejected indefinitely by the structural fence.

The cutoff now concerns only capabilities actually admitted while `ACTIVE`.

Its wording is epistemically honest:

> any admitted operation has completed, compensated or committed, or is presumed abandoned.

“Presumed abandoned” is materially weaker than “proved impossible” and must remain exactly that weak.

The two post-cutoff empty scans then support the stated point-in-time claim:

- no reachable DynamoDB business state;
- no residue observed in any inventoried S3 scope;
- at the time of those completed scans.

That claim does not promise permanent physical zero.

The recurring sweeper supplies the missing temporal coverage.

For the Round G counterexample:

1. An operation is admitted while `ACTIVE`.
2. Final purge and verification run.
3. The operation later creates an S3 object.
4. Its compensation fails or never executes.
5. A later scheduled sweep finds the object.
6. The object is repurged.
7. The discovery raises an alarm/finding.

Therefore, late residue is neither invisible nor silently normalized.

The solution provides eventual remediation plus operational evidence, rather than claiming an impossible one-time permanent proof.

That is a real closure.

One wording correction is important: the mechanism is not literally a “permanent sweeper” if tenant-specific scanning stops after 90 days.

The durable implementation pattern is permanent.

Each deleted tenant’s enhanced sweep eligibility is bounded to 90 days.

That distinction should appear in the design.

### Is the existing 90-day LGPD ceiling sufficient justification?

It is a legitimate normative ceiling, but it should not be the only derivation.

`privacy-lgpd.md` establishes that no residual copy may survive beyond 90 days.

That justifies 90 days as the outer remediation deadline and maximum monitoring window.

It does not, by itself, prove that no admitted capability can first materialize residue on day 91.

That proof comes from the independently inventoried capability horizons:

- presigned URL expiry;
- Lambda termination;
- Step Functions execution timeout;
- any provider-specific asynchronous completion horizon;
- any other bearer capability capable of producing in-scope state.

The design should therefore record the logic as:

`tenant sweep window ≥ maximum late-materialization horizon + detection/remediation margin`

and separately:

`tenant sweep window ≤ normative 90-day residual-copy ceiling`

Given the stated maximum horizon of 1800 seconds, a daily sweep retained for 90 days overwhelmingly satisfies the first relationship.

The existing 90-day rule supplies the privacy ceiling; the execution/capability inventory supplies the safety justification.

This is a documentation obligation, not a remaining architecture choice.

The two scans must also be full successful scans:

- every DynamoDB scope;
- every inventoried S3 prefix;
- complete pagination;
- no suppressed access or listing error;
- no transition to `DELETED` on partial or indeterminate results.

## 2. Discoverable failed compensation — CLOSED

Reusing the same sweeper is the correct design.

A separate compensation-recovery subsystem would add mechanism without adding a stronger invariant.

Including both clean and quarantine prefixes closes the concrete failure modes:

- compensating delete returned an error;
- the process died before issuing the delete;
- an external write became visible after final verification;
- a retry recreated an object before its admitted capability expired.

Alarm-on-find is essential and is present.

The finding should retain tenant, bucket, key classification, discovery time, purge outcome, and correlation/run identity where available.

That is an implementation/audit requirement, not an open design gap.

## 3. Two-phase `ExtractionRun` marker rollout — CLOSED

The Phase A/Phase B sequencing is now migration-safe.

Phase A makes all new qualifying runs marker-bearing before enforcement exists.

The idempotent backfill covers older qualifying runs.

Repeat-until-zero strongly consistent verification correctly avoids treating paginated `Scan` as a database snapshot.

Only after verified convergence does Phase B enable:

- the lifecycle fence;
- marker-required retry;
- rejection/reconciliation of markerless runs.

Permanently retaining markerless reconciliation prevents migration assumptions from becoming an unmonitored invariant.

The three named implementation obligations are necessary and sufficient at design level:

- the fenced executor owns transaction construction and the lifecycle entry’s stable identity;
- typed DynamoDB cancellation reasons survive mapping to the compensation caller;
- convergence is asserted from eligible markerless rows, never inferred from aggregate counts.

These must become executable tests and deployment gates, but they no longer require another architecture round.

## Remaining status

No further prose reconciliation is likely to improve the architecture materially.

Remaining work is now:

- implementation;
- migration rehearsal;
- infrastructure verification;
- alarm/runbook validation;
- empirical testing of complete scans and late-residue repair;
- the explicit SES human decision.

Those are real gates, but they are not unresolved review-only design questions.

Accordingly, another review-only round is not warranted.
